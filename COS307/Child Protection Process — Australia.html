Child Protection in Australia

How the system works

FOR TRAINING USE

In Australia, each state and territory runs its own statutory child protection system. Departments respond to children who have been — or are at risk of being — abused, neglected, or otherwise harmed, or whose families can't provide adequate care. Delivery is shared: government departments run the statutory process, while non-government organisations mostly deliver the support services families are referred into.

Anyone can raise a concern about a child's welfare — a neighbour, a teacher, a doctor, police, a relative, or the child themselves. What happens next depends on screening: department staff weigh the report against a threshold that decides whether it needs a statutory child protection response, or is better met by a support service.

National data only captures reports that clear that threshold and become a child protection notification. Reports about things like financial hardship or social isolation, with no immediate protection concern, are usually redirected as a family support issue instead — and never enter the national statistics at all.

Because each jurisdiction sets its own threshold, the same set of facts can be screened differently in different states — this is one of the biggest sources of variation in the national data.

Moving forward. If you're heading into placement or a community services role: practise noticing your own instinct to either over-react (jump to removal) or under-react (dismiss a concern) — both showed up in these cases. Get comfortable asking "what does screening need to establish here?" before deciding anything, and always ask what happens to a family after a case is closed or a report is redirected — that follow-through is where real cases most often fall through.
